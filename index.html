What it does

Every source, its own file

Add microphones, outputs (speakers, headphones: everything your computer plays) and webcams, and record them all at once. Each becomes its own file, so you can mix, cut and fix them separately later. Add or remove one while recording: added back, it carries on in the same file.

Lossless audio, video on the GPU

Each audio track is 24-bit FLAC at the device's own sample rate, with Windows' audio effects bypassed where the device allows. Each webcam is H.264 in an MP4, encoded by your graphics card (by Windows' software encoder if it has none).

In sync, however long

Every device's clock runs a little fast or slow, so separate recordings drift apart, by seconds over an evening. Black Box measures each device against the system clock and corrects it. Video is timed by the same clock. A gap, lost audio or a webcam removed for a while, stays a gap of the right length: silence, or a NO VIDEO picture. The files line up from start to end.

Safe through a crash

Audio and video go to a journal on disk first, flushed every second. If Black Box, Windows or the power dies, blackbox recover finishes the files from it, losing at most the last second.

Light on CPU, because it has to be

Audio arrives in real time and doesn't wait: a recorder that falls behind loses it. So low CPU isn't a nicety, it's what keeps the recording whole while everything else on the machine runs. Capture runs at Windows' real-time priority and does little more than copy; the graphics card encodes video where it can, and audio compression runs in the background at low priority.

See what you're getting

Live levels for every track while you record, and for every device before you pick it. Clips are flagged, and each webcam counts the frames it has recorded and dropped. The top bar tells you how much recording time the disk has left. The window collapses to a single bar, can stay on top, and keeps itself out of screen captures.

Recovering a recording

A recording interrupted by a crash or a power cut stays as its journal. Finish it with the command line tool, which the installer puts on your PATH:

> blackbox recover "C:\Users\you\AppData\Local\BlackBox\rec\2026-11-17 10-30-36" C:\Users\you\AppData\Local\BlackBox\rec\2026-11-17 10-30-36: finished

If the journal is damaged, it says how and removes nothing.
